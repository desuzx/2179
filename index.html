Chapter Per Capita 02

A Different Perspective

Raw prize money favours large countries simply because they have more players. However, if we divide by the population, it reveals a different story.

Denmark ranks #1 in per capita, earning over $10 million per million people. Nearly 4x more than the United States.

Nordic countries like Denmark, Finland and Sweden are ranked highly, which is interesting given their small populations.

Chapter Growth & Genre 03

A Billion Dollar Industry

From its start, esports has transitioned from a niche hobby to a global phenomenon. While total prize pools grew consistently since 2010, the mix of games driving that growth has changed over the years as new genres emerged. The dip in 2020  is likely due to the COVID-19 pandemic, which caused many events to be cancelled or postponed, additionally in 2024 the reason why it is decreasing is likely due to the fact that the data is incomplete as the data only goes until October 2024
Global Esports Prize Money Over Time
Hover the numbered yellow pins for context behind each turning point
• Strategy: Early esports (1998 to 2010) was mainly only titles like StarCraft, however esports was not as mainstream yet.
• First-Person Shooter: CS:GO, has maintained a steady share throughout the years, it has been consistent within the competitive scene for over a decade.
• MOBA: Dota 2 and League of Legends became massively popular in the 2010s, pushing esports to become mainstream.
• Battle Royale: Since 2017, games like Fortnite and PUBG have quickly risen in popularity, contributing a significant share of prize money in just a few years, an example of this is the 2019 Fortnite World Cup which had a prize pool of $30 million, and one sole winner won $3 million.
• Sports: Led by titles such as FIFA and Rocket League. While it has not reached the highs comapred to the other genres, it does have a consistent playerbase

Chapter Biggest Events 04

Tournament Prizes

While many games do have a active competetive scene, the biggest prize pools within the esports scence is currently being dominated by one game and one tournament. Dota 2 The International, claiming 10 out of 20 spots. The International is an annual world championship tournament for Dota 2 which only happens once per year. And for the Internation 2021 tournament, it had a massive $40 million as it's prize pool, making it the biggest esports tournament prize pool wise in history.
Additionally, out of the top 20 tournaments, 17 of them  fall within the MOBA genre, being Dota 2,   League of Legends  and Arena of Valor, highlighting how dominant the MOBA genre is within the esports scene. There are only 2 non-MOBA games to be in the top 20, being Fortnite and PUBG.
The (🇦🇺) ana wins  label on the chart marks the International 2018 and 2019, won by Australian player Anathan "ana" Pham, making him the only player to win The International twice  and the only Australian player to ever win The International.

Chapter The Players 05

The World's Highest Earning Players

The top 100 highest-earning players in esports history are only located within 27 countries. China has the most amount of players, with 24 players in the list, followed by the United States (11) and Denmark (9). Australia has two players  in the top 100.
From the map, we notice that most of the highest-earning players are located within Europe, this is mainly because the top 20 Highest-Earning Esports Players are all Dota 2 players, and since Dota 2 has the biggest prize pools every year, and it being a game that has been dominated by the European and Chinese  teams. China stands out as it is the largest single bubble  with 24 players in the top 100. Additionally, Australia , still manages to have 2 players within the top 100 mainly because both of these players are Dota 2 competitors, where 1 of them won a Dota 2 International.
